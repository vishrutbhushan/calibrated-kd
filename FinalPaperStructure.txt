Key Empirical Findings:
- Regime A (Student Questions, TREC-6, App Reviews): Fine-grained semantics and ordinal boundaries where CKD standalone dominates (+1.35 pp to +9.58 pp lead over Post-Hoc). On all three, applying Post-Hoc actively degrades student accuracy.
- Regime B (Twitter Emotion, TweetEval Emotion, BBC News): Coarse/social domains where CKD matches or beats Post-Hoc standalone, and CKD + Post-Hoc Combined delivers decisive peak performance (hitting 93.10% on BBC News).

9) Limitations
-> Primary benefit is observed on naturally skewed, fine-grained, and ordinal multi-class tasks where prior bias starves minority gradients. On simple, balanced binary tasks, baseline methods already perform close to the teacher ceiling.
-> Dependence on baseline teacher competence: If a zero-shot teacher achieves near-random performance (<20% accuracy on a domain), calibration cannot recover non-existent knowledge.
-> Reliance on single-token verbalizers (A, B, C...) and prompt sensitivity to the null token representation.

11) Appendix / Ablations
1. Cross-Architecture & Student Model Scaling:
   - Comparing Bidirectional Encoder (RoBERTa-base, 125M) vs. Causal Decoders (Qwen2.5-0.5B and Qwen3-4B with LoRA).
   - Shows CKD gains scale consistently across both encoder and decoder student architectures.
2. Multi-Task Distillation with Unified Student Backbone:
   - Single shared student learning multiple diverse tasks simultaneously (TweetEval + BBC News, BBC News + TREC-6, Twitter Emotion + TREC-6, and 5-Task Unified).
   - Demonstrates that CKD prevents cross-task negative transfer and minority class starvation in shared representation spaces.
3. Hard Pseudo-Labels vs. Soft Distillation:
   - Disentangling teacher probability confidence from prior-bias contamination.
4. Computational Efficiency Comparison:
   - Permutation Averaging (requiring O(K!) prompt permutations) vs. CKD (requiring exactly ONE single null forward pass at zero marginal training cost).
5. Multi-Seed Robustness:
   - Variance and standard deviation analysis across multiple random seeds confirming statistical stability.
